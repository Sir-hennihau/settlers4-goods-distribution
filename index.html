How Settlers IV Distributes Goods

The percentage sliders are real, but they are not a ratio of deliveries. They are a divisor in a debt ledger that is shared between goods and never reset — which is why coal and bread land where you didn't ask them to.

Start here: what the sliders actually promise

A slider does not say “send 25% of the coal here.” It says “this kind of building should end up having received 25% of all the coal I have ever handed out.” The game keeps a tally of what it has given each kind of building, and every time a lump of coal comes free it gives it to whoever is furthest behind their promise.

That one difference — a promise measured over the whole match, rather than a ratio applied right now — is where every surprise comes from.

One lump of coal, start to finish

  1. A lump of coal comes free

    Somewhere in your settlement a coal pile is sitting with nobody assigned to carry it. About once a second at normal game speed, the game picks one up and asks where it should go. Every good gets exactly one of these decisions per round — coal gets two, because it is special-cased in the code.

  2. Stage 1 decides what kind of building gets it

    This is the only step your sliders touch. The game runs down the list of coal consumers — gold smelter, iron smelter, toolsmith, weaponsmith, arrow factory — and for each one asks: how much has this kind received so far, compared to the share I promised it? Whichever kind is furthest behind wins. A kind set to 0%, one you have paused, or one whose buildings are all full is skipped.

  3. Stage 2 decides which building of that kind gets it

    Your sliders are finished; they have no say left. If stage 1 said “a weaponsmith,” stage 2 looks at all your weaponsmiths and picks the one that is emptiest and closest to the coal. Emptiness counts roughly twice as much as anything already on its way. Any building whose input pile already holds 8 coal, counting deliveries in transit, is refused outright.

  4. The delivery is written down

    The winning kind of building gets +1 on its tally. Not the individual building — the kind. That tally is exactly the number stage 1 will weigh against your slider next time round.

So how do the two stages relate?

Stage 1 obeys you. Stage 2 ignores you. Stage 1 is the fairness system, and it is the only place your percentages exist. Stage 2 is pure logistics — geography and warehouse space — and it runs identically no matter what the sliders say. If coal is reaching the right type of building but always the same one of them, that is stage 2 and no slider will change it. If coal is reaching the wrong type entirely, that is stage 1, and the tally is why.

What the tally is really doing

The tally is the whole mechanism, and it has three properties nobody would guess from looking at the panel:

  • There is one tally per kind of building — not one per good. A weaponsmith has a single tally, and both coal and iron bars add to it. So feeding it iron makes it look “already served” to the coal system, and its coal dries up. Mines have one tally covering bread, meat and fish together.
  • The tally is never wiped. It runs for the entire match and is saved with your game. Move a slider an hour in and the game does not change the flow — it changes the target, then spends thousands of deliveries paying off the imbalance you just created before things settle.
  • Only goods that have a slider are written down. Iron ore and gold ore have no slider anywhere in the panel, so delivering them adds nothing to the tally. This is why gold and iron smelters quietly win the competition for coal: coal is the only thing on their tab.

The rest of this page is the evidence: the exact formulas, the memory they live in, the instructions that implement them, and measured results from re-running the algorithm.

The two decisions, in full

Every delivery is two separate choices, made by two different formulas. Only the first one looks at your sliders.

Stage 1 — which building type

The slider is a divisor, not a share

score = (counter[type] × 256 + 128)
        ÷ sliderPercent[type]

→ lowest score wins

counter[type] is a running tally of goods already delivered to that building type. It is stored per economy sector, it is 16-bit, and it is never reset for the whole match.

Stage 2 — which building instance

Then stock and distance decide

score = 262 144 000
      × (16 − incoming − 2×inStock)
      ÷ hexDistance

→ highest score wins

Distance is measured to the nearest pile of that good, not to a storehouse. A building whose input pile already holds inStock + incoming ≥ 8 is rejected outright.

The critical detail

The ledger at +0x1C4 is indexed by building type alone. There is no good-type dimension. One weaponsmith counter is incremented by coal deliveries and by iron-bar deliveries. One coal-mine counter is incremented by bread, meat and fish. The sliders for different goods are therefore competing for the same number.

The dispatch loop

Runs once every 16 logic ticks, for every economy sector on the map. The main loop at 0x513C80 paces a tick to 0x47 = 71 ms at default speed (47 ms at speed 2, then 71/(n−1)), so a cycle is about 1.1 s of real time — and that is also a hard ceiling of one delivery per good per cycle, two for coal.

Reconstructed The shipped routine is 2,528 bytes of x86 with no source available. What follows is pseudocode written from the disassembly — accurate to the control flow and the arithmetic, but not the game's own text. The verbatim instructions behind the two load-bearing claims are printed below it.

// CEcoSector::DistributeGoods — 0x004D5D30
// gated by (gameManager[0x30] & 0xF) == 0xF   →  1 in 16 ticks

for rank in 1..42:
    good = first good whose transportRank == rank      // +0x228
    if requestSet[good] is empty: continue

    ── stage 1 · pick the consumer building TYPE ──
    best = INF ; chosen = none
    for (type, percent) in sliders[good]:          // ascending type id
        if percent == 0: continue                   // 0% is fully excluded
        score = (counter[type]*256 + 128) / percent
        if score >= best: continue
        if no active building of `type` is requesting: continue
        best = score ; chosen = type

    ── stage 2 · pick WHICH building of that type ──
    for b in buildings(chosen) that are requesting:
        src  = nearestPileOf(good, b.pos)
        dist = hexDistance(b.pos, src.pos)
        s    = 262144000 * (16 - b.incoming - 2*b.inStock) / dist
    target = argmax(s)
    if target.inStock + target.incoming >= 8: continue   // pile cap

    if createTransportJob(src, target):
        counter[chosen] += 1                        // 0x4D664C — the only write

    if good == COAL and firstPass: redo this good once  // coal gets 2 jobs/cycle
    clear requestSet[good]

The feedback loop that breaks it

Stage 1 divides by your slider — but the thing it divides is written by every good that shares a consumer.

0x4D5E6E0x4D662B

Which deliveries actually count

Not everything a building receives touches the ledger — and this is the part that decides who wins.

A gold smelter also eats gold ore, and an iron smelter also eats iron ore. Those are big, constant flows, so the obvious question is why they don't inflate the smelters' counters the same way iron bars inflate the weaponsmith's.

The answer is that ore has no slider. The supply-priority map holds exactly 35 pairs, covering exactly the eight goods in the panel's top row. Ore, stone, boards, tools, weapons, sulfur and the rest appear nowhere in it. When the dispatcher reaches such a good, UpdateGoodDistributionValues returns an empty list, and the branch at 0x4D5E6E jumps clean over stage 1: the chosen type stays at -1, every requesting building is accepted as a candidate, and the good is routed by stage 2 alone — nearest and emptiest wins.

The increment at 0x4D664C is guarded by test eax,eax / js on that same -1. So a good without a slider can never advance the counter. There is exactly one instruction in the binary that writes it, and it is unreachable unless stage 1 picked a type.

So which goods share a counter with coal?

Only the ones that have a coal-consumer in their own slider table. Per building type, using the game's shipped defaults:

Why your sliders lie

Six independent mechanisms, each verified in the disassembly. They compound.

shared ledger

One counter per building type, shared by every good that has a slider

The ledger is indexed by building type only. Every sliderable good delivered to a consumer bumps the same number, so the iron slider spends the weaponsmith's coal budget and the fish slider spends the gold mine's bread budget. Goods with no slider — ore above all — bypass the ledger entirely.

no reset

The ledger is cumulative for the entire match

It is zeroed only in the sector's constructor and it is written into the savegame. Moving a slider does not change the flow now — it changes the target the historical debt is measured against, so the engine first spends thousands of units repaying the imbalance you just created.

zero debt

A consumer that receives nothing accrues no debt

Types sitting at 0%, paused, or simply not built yet keep a counter of zero. The moment they become eligible their score is near-zero, so they take everything until they catch up. Build an arrow factory late and it drinks all your coal.

per type

The percentage belongs to the type, not the building

Stage 1 picks a building type; stage 2 only chooses which instance. Two weaponsmiths on a 25% coal slider share 25% between them — 12.5% each — while a single gold smelter keeps its full 25%.

free skip

Ineligible types are skipped without paying

A type whose buildings are all full (input pile at 8), paused, or not requesting is passed over — and its counter does not advance. It builds up a deficit while it waits, then claims a burst of deliveries the moment it frees up.

stage 2

Distance and stock override nothing — but decide everything else

Once the type is fixed, the slider has no further say. The instance is chosen by (16 − incoming − 2×inStock) ÷ distance to the nearest pile of that good, so a near-empty, nearby building wins every time.

practicewhat to do

Working with it instead of against it

  • Budget across goods, not within one. Before setting a bread slider, look at what meat and fish are doing to the same mines. A mine on 100% fish will take almost no bread no matter where you drag the bread slider.
  • Set your ratios early. The ledger is cumulative, so a slider set in the first minutes behaves as advertised; the same slider set an hour in has thousands of units of history to fight.
  • Expect a burst after any change. Whatever you raise will be over-served until its debt catches up, then it settles. That transient is the system working, not a bug.
  • Count buildings, not sliders. A type's percentage is split across all its buildings, so adding a second weaponsmith halves each one's coal — it does not double the type's share.
  • Expect the smelters to win coal. Their ore intake carries no slider and so never touches the ledger. Their counters stay clean, and they out-compete the weaponsmith, toolsmith and arrow factory for coal every time — dial the smelters down if you want the smiths fed.
  • Remember the sliders are per economy sector. If your settlement is split, each part carries its own sliders and its own ledger; the panel edits whichever sector you are looking at.
  • Keep consumers close to their supply. Stage 2 divides by hex distance to the nearest pile, so a distant building of the right type loses to a near one every cycle.

The ceiling on your army

Before any slider matters, the dispatcher itself is a hard throughput limit — and it is the number your community already measures as Ausreizung Tragelimit.

One cycle is 16 ticks at 71 ms = 1.136 s, and it emits two coal jobs and one job of every other good, per economy sector. That is:

Per economy sector

Coal: 105.63 units / min

Every other good, sliderable or not: 52.82 / min. Coal gets double because of the second-pass special case at 0x4D6506.

A tier-3 soldier costs

4 coal and 2 gold ore

2 gold bars (2 coal + 2 ore), 1 iron bar (1 coal), 1 weapon (1 coal). Coal caps you at 105.63/4; gold ore at 52.82/2. Both land on the same number.

Absolute ceiling

26.4 T3 / min / sector

1,584 an hour. 1,000 soldiers by 1:40 needs ≈19.4/min sustained over the last 40 minutes — 73 % of the ceiling. Reaching it needs a second economy sector, which carries its own budget.

Do the community patches lift it?

No — they lift a different gate, and that is precisely what makes this one bind. There are two, in series:

Gate 1 · a rate · 0x4F2770

2 coal jobs per cycle

105.63 coal/min per sector, hard-coded in the tick gate. Never patched. No number of idle carriers buys a third job in a cycle.

Gate 2 · a supply · 0x4D4AF0

A free carrier must exist

Each job hunts the sector's carrier list for the nearest one with the active flag set. None free → the job returns 0, the cycle slot is burned, and the ledger is not advanced.

In the base game the settler cap kept the carrier pool small, so gate 2 bound first — that is the "only so many goods could be carried" behaviour. Settlers United raises the entity counts (PatchMoreSettlersAndBuildings, PatchIncreaseBuildingLimit, PatchFerryLimit, IncreasedSelectionLimit, all named in the plugin), so with enough settlers you can keep a carrier free for every job. Gate 2 stops binding — and gate 1 is what you hit instead.

So your sheet's number does not go stale under Settlers United. It gets more accurate, because only now can a build actually reach it.

One consolation in the failure path: because a carrier-starved job never reaches the increment at 0x4D664C, a carrier shortage costs you throughput but does not distort the ratios. The ledger only records deliveries that really happened, so your sliders still mean what they mean — you simply get fewer of everything.

Verified against the installed plugins: SettlersUnited.asi, S4HD-Patch.asi, S4WarriorsLib.asi, ExtraZoom.asi, S4HD-Manager.dll, S4ModApi.dll and ddraw.dll contain no reference — as a virtual address, an RVA, or a .text-relative offset — to 0x4D5D30, the &0xF gate at 0x4F2770, the 71 ms pacing at 0x513C80, the ledger increment, or the coal second-pass. The gate bytes 83 E6 0F are intact on disk, and the only transport-related strings in the whole plugin are about the transport priority preset UI, which writes the rank array at +0x228 and changes no rate.

So the direction of the effect is the opposite of what you might expect. In the unpatched game a low carrier cap could bind below 105.63 coal/min. Raising it removes that, which leaves the dispatcher as the binding ceiling — the patches make this number more relevant, not less.

How to falsify this A build sitting at 101 % should visibly stall: coal permanently short, the smelters never all fed at once, and idle capacity in whichever type the ledger is starving. If a patched game runs the base Trojan plan without stalling, something did lift the rate and these figures need re-measuring. The one caveat I cannot close statically is a signature-scanning patch that locates the code by byte pattern rather than address — though nothing in the plugin's own list of patch names suggests one.

The way past the ceiling is not more carriers but more economy sectors: the driver at 0x4F27C9 walks an array of up to 16,383 sectors and calls the dispatcher once for each, so every separate economy area carries its own full budget of 2 coal jobs per cycle.

always onthe toolsmith

Keeping the toolsmith running

Tools are growth — builders, diggers, woodcutters, miners. A stalled toolsmith costs more than a stalled weaponsmith, so it should never be the thing that starves. The common 40 % setting is right, and the mechanism explains why it is also nearly free.

A fat toolsmith slider does not take 40 % of your coal

One Roman toolsmith consumes 2.74 coal a minute. That is 2.6 % of the dispatcher's 105.63/min ceiling. It cannot physically absorb 40 %: its input pile caps at 8, and a full building is skipped without cost, so the surplus flows straight past it to the smelters. Two toolsmiths still only want 5.2 %.

So 40 on the toolsmith does not mean "40 % of my coal goes to tools". It means "the toolsmith is first in the queue whenever it has room" — which is exactly the property you want, and at full coal supply it costs nothing at all. Simulating the Roman planner build from minute 30 to minute 100:

The thing that actually starves it is iron bars

Look at the last row. Coal slider 40, iron-bar slider 13 — and uptime collapses back to 73.5 %. Breaking the stalls down by cause, at a 6 coal slider the toolsmith is dry on coal only 2.7 % of cycles but dry on iron bars 24.8 % of them.

This is the single most common way the toolsmith dies, and it is invisible: you see coal piled up outside a toolsmith that is not working, conclude the coal slider is fine, and never look at the other panel. A tool costs one coal and one iron bar, and the iron-bar panel has its own ledger with the weaponsmith taking 87 % of it by default.

Be generous on both panels. The toolsmith needs about 2.74 bars/min out of the ~21 your iron smelts produce — 13 % — so a 40 % iron-bar slider is self-limiting in exactly the same way, and equally free.

When a fat slider still fails

  • Thin iron-bar slider. By far the most common. Widen both panels or neither.
  • Set to 0. A 0 % type is excluded from the lookup outright, keeps its debt frozen forever, and can never work its way back. There is no situation in which 0 on the toolsmith is correct.
  • Cutting it mid-game. Lowering the slider after a tool rush freezes the toolsmith for thousands of deliveries — see the debt arithmetic below. A fat slider that you never touch avoids this entirely.
  • Genuine coal collapse. Robust: at 35 % of normal coal supply the fat setting still holds 96.9 % uptime. It only fails once total coal drops below the toolsmith's own 2.74/min — about half of one coal mine.
  • Carrier shortage. Gate 2 — the job fails and the slot is burned no matter what the slider says.
  • Wrong economy sector. Sliders and ledger are per sector. A toolsmith in a sector with no coal cannot be helped by any setting.
  • Distance. Stage 2 divides by hex distance to the nearest pile, so a toolsmith far from coal refills more slowly and burns its 8-deep buffer — roughly 3 minutes of production — during any interruption.

Does the opening need different numbers?

It looks like it should. Your first 200 soldiers arrive between minute 35 and 60 at roughly 8 T3/min, and at that scale a single toolsmith is 0.34 tools per weapon — more than twice the 0.15 of a mature 18/min economy. And switching is not free: moving the iron slider from 16 down to 14, with gold and weapon going 22 → 23, around the 200-soldier mark costs 702 deliveries, about 7 minutes with the iron smelter frozen, to buy nothing at all. So set the early-weighted numbers on turn one and leave them.

The general rule, to derive it for a different tool count: with the toolsmith self-limiting, split whatever is left after its slider across gold, iron and weapon as 2 : (1 + ρ) : 2, where ρ is tools per weapon. Early ρ ≈ 0.34, late ≈ 0.15, and the whole span from 4 to 26 T3/min only moves the iron slider between 18 and 13.

Ceiling warning 1000 by 1:40 does not fit in one sector

Your milestones imply 200 T3 between minute 35 and 60 (8/min), 200 more by 80 (10/min), then 600 in the last twenty minutes — 30 per minute. That last figure needs 125.5 coal a minute, which is 119 % of the 105.63/min a single economy sector can dispatch. No slider setting reaches it; the simulation tops out at 846 by minute 100 and gets to 1000 around minute 110.

Three ways out, in order of practicality: start the military ramp earlier so the curve is flatter and never demands 30/min; split the settlement into two economy sectors, each of which gets its own full dispatch budget; or accept 1:50 instead of 1:40. The first is free and is what the schedule above assumes you will do.

0x4D5EADthe debt

Tweaking mid-game: paying the debt

In plain words

The slider is not a tap you open and close. It is a finish line for the whole match. When you move it, the game does not change the flow — it changes the target and then sprints at it. Whichever building type is now ahead of its new share receives absolutely nothing until the others have caught up.

So a slider change never takes effect immediately, and the bigger the change, the longer the game spends in a state that looks nothing like your sliders. Nothing is wasted — the totals do come out right in the end — but if you keep reacting to shortages by nudging sliders, you spend the entire game in transients and never see the ratio you asked for.

The safe way to use them: decide what the whole game should look like — how many soldiers, how many tools — set the sliders to that once, and leave them alone. The early tool rush still happens by itself, because the gold smelter and weaponsmith don't exist yet and the game skips building types that can't accept anything.

In detail

The arithmetic

Stage 1 always drives the ledger toward N(b) ∝ p(b). That is the whole behaviour: the sliders describe the cumulative split the engine is aiming at, and the instantaneous flow only equals the slider ratio once the ledger has converged there.

Change the sliders when the ledger stands at N⁰, and convergence needs N⁰(b) + Δ(b) = λ·p(b) with every Δ(b) ≥ 0 — deliveries can only be added, never taken back. That forces

λ = maxb ( N⁰(b) / p(b) ) D = 100·λ − Σb N⁰(b) deliveries before the new ratio governs

The type that attains that maximum is the one that is over-served, and it receives nothing at all for the whole of D. Everyone else shares D between them in proportion to their sliders.

The trap, with numbers

Rush 300 tools before building any military. The ledger is then goldsmelter 0, ironsmelter 300, weaponsmith 0, toolsmith 600 — remember a tool costs the toolsmith 2 ledger points, one for coal and one for the iron bar. Now switch to the 1,000-soldier setting (gold 34, iron 22, tool 10, weapon 34):

λ = max( gold 0/34 , iron 300/22 , tool 600/10 , weapon 0/34 ) = 59 D = 100 × 59 − 900 = 5,000 deliveries

At roughly 99 deliveries a minute that is 51 minutes during which the toolsmith receives no coal and no iron whatsoever. Players read that as "my toolsmith is broken." It is the ledger repaying a debt you took out during the rush.

Set that same row at minute 0 instead and the cost is zero. Simulating a Roman opening — 2 iron smelts and 1 toolsmith for 30 minutes, then the full build — the set-once run and the rush-then-switch run produce identical output (81 tools at the switch, 250 tools and 1,259 T3 at 100 minutes), because during the rush the sliders are irrelevant: the gold smelter and weaponsmith don't exist, and ineligible types are skipped for free. The rush is given to you by the eligibility rule, not by the sliders.

The one setting that does real damage is dropping the toolsmith to 0 after the rush (gold 40, iron 20, tool 0, weapon 40). Same simulation: tools collapse from 250 to 90 and 163 iron bars pile up unused, because a 0 % type is excluded outright and can never work off its debt.

Reading your own ledger

You cannot see the counters, but you can reconstruct all four exactly from numbers the game shows you:

  • Gold smelter = 2 × (T3 soldiers built) + gold bars in store
  • Weaponsmith = 2 × [ (T3 soldiers built) + weapons in store ]
  • Iron smelter = weapons made + tools made + iron bars in store
  • Toolsmith = 2 × (tools made)

Divide each by its slider. If the four numbers are equal, the ledger has converged and you are getting the ratio you set. If one is far higher than the others, that type is the one currently being starved — and the formula above tells you how many deliveries are left to go.

Source note The per-building production speeds are from your sheet, not the binary — buildingInfo.xml only carries a small productiondelay, with the real cycle length spread across the job animations in jobInfo.xml. What is verified here from the shipped files is the 71 ms tick, the 16-tick dispatch cycle, the double coal pass, every input recipe, and the ledger arithmetic. Since your table reproduces the tick rate to four decimals, any patch drift in it is small — but the slider formulas above depend only on the recipes, so they hold regardless.

The write path, for anyone patching it

A slider drag is normalised to a sum of 100 in 0x4EBDA0, packed into a network event, and applied in CNetInputProcessor::Process at 0x4F6748, which calls the map setter at 0x4C5330 with (buildingType, goodType, percent). The single instruction that advances the ledger is inc word ptr [ebx+eax*2+0x1C4] at 0x4D664C.
